Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "Rayfield-Based Calibration and Effective Physical
Model Identification of a Common Main Objective Stereo Microscope" for
consideration in Optics and Lasers in Engineering.

Common Main Objective (CMO) stereo microscopes are widely used in optical
microscopy and experimental mechanics, but their shared-objective architecture
violates the single-optical-centre assumption underlying standard stereo
calibration. This produces severe optics-pose coupling and makes conventional
central-camera calibration unreliable on real CMO data, where OpenCV stereo
calibration exceeds 300 px reprojection error.

The manuscript proposes a two-stage optical calibration methodology. First, a
per-pixel Zernike rayfield is fitted to calibration observations. Second, this
measured rayfield is used as a diagnostic observable to identify a compact,
physically interpretable CMO model: residual modal analysis guides the model
construction from a naive perspective model to a quasi-telecentric CMO skeleton
with per-channel SE(3) arm alignment. On real open Pycaso data the resulting
26-parameter model reaches 1.06 px reprojection error, and refines to 0.28 px
when used to initialise a Schur-stabilised free-pose bundle adjustment, within
0.1 px of a black-box Soloff polynomial floor (0.18 px) while remaining
physically interpretable.

We believe the manuscript fits the scope of Optics and Lasers in Engineering
because it addresses a practical optical-engineering problem in microscopy
calibration, combines optical modelling with computational imaging, and
provides a reproducible method for identifying interpretable physical models in
non-central optical instruments. We compare against both OpenCV and the Soloff
polynomial baseline. An external profilometry experiment is presented as a
consistency check of the calibrated reconstructions; absolute traceable depth
accuracy on a certified 3D artefact is explicitly identified as future work,
and no absolute metrological claim is made.

Code, data links, intermediate states, figure-generation scripts, and a Zenodo
archive are provided to support full reproducibility. The manuscript is not
under consideration elsewhere.
